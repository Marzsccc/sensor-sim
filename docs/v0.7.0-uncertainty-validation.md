# v0.7.0 — Display-Time Uncertainty Propagation (预测不确定度椭圆)

**Date:** 2026-08-13 (autonomous session)

## What was added

New module `PredictorUncertainty` in `sensor_sim/predict.py` — propagates
the state *covariance* through the display-time pose prediction so the
AR-HUD can render an uncertainty ellipse around AR markers at display time.

The display-time pose prediction (v0.5.0 `PosePredictor`) answers *where
the vehicle will be* at display time; v0.7.0 answers *how sure we are*.
Both are needed for production AR-HUD rendering: markers are faded,
jittered, or clamped when the confidence region exceeds a lane width.

## Design

Display-time covariance = initial-state uncertainty + process/model noise:

```
P_disp = F^n P_init F^{n,T} + Σ_{k=1..n} F^{n-k} Q F^{n-k,T}
```

- **Initial-state term** — the ESKF error covariance P restricted to the
  9×9 [p, v, θ] block, propagated linearly through the same first-order
  error-state dynamics F used inside the ESKF prediction:
  `F = [[I, I·dt, 0], [0, I, −R[a]×·dt], [0, 0, I − [ω]×·dt]]`.
- **Process-noise term** — IMU white noise (velocity random walk,
  angular random walk), bias random walks, and a **modeling-error term**
  `σ_m` (default 0.5 m/s² per axis) that accounts for the
  constant-acceleration assumption breaking down over the horizon
  (jerk/steering change). This is the dominant term at automotive speeds:
  a 0.5 m/s² unmodeled jerk produces ~6 mm of position std at 80 ms —
  small in absolute terms, but the *reason* the ellipse exists. At
  20 m/s it is equivalent to ~1-2 m of cross-track uncertainty after a
  steering event, i.e. lane-level.

The horizontal 2×2 position covariance is decomposed into the standard
ellipse parameters (major/minor axes, rotation, area, 95% circular
radius) for direct rendering.

## API

```python
from sensor_sim.predict import PredictorUncertainty, UncertaintyConfig, PosePredictor, PredictionConfig

unc = PredictorUncertainty(UncertaintyConfig(model_error_acc_std=0.5))
pred = PosePredictor(PredictionConfig(use_wheel=True))

res = unc.predict_with_covariance(
    pred,
    pos=eskf.position, vel=eskf.velocity, att=eskf.quaternion,
    acc_meas=acc_m, gyr_meas=gyr_m,
    horizon=0.08,                    # display horizon (s)
    P_init=eskf.state.P[:9, :9],     # ESKF 9x9 [p,v,theta] covariance
    wheel_speed=10.0, wheel_yaw_rate=0.0,
)
# res["P_disp"]  -> 9x9 display-time covariance
# res["ellipse"] -> {sigma_x, sigma_y, rotation_deg, area, radius_95}
```

## Validation

Run `examples/uncertainty_demo.py` — ESKF run to steady state on a
straight + turn trajectory, then 80 ms propagation:

| Quantity | ESKF settled | After 80 ms prediction |
|---|---|---|
| pos std (x/y) | 0.210 m | 0.211 / 0.211 m |
| vel std | 0.037 m/s | 0.147 m/s |
| heading std | 0.118° | 0.119° |
| 1σ ellipse area | — | 0.139 m² |
| 95% radius | — | 0.515 m |

Observations:

1. With a tactical IMU and 80 ms horizon, the *initial-state* ESKF
   uncertainty (0.21 m) dominates; the process-noise growth is ~6 mm
   (model error) — the ellipse is mostly the fusion uncertainty, not the
   prediction.
2. With no model error (`model_error_acc_std=0`), pure IMU noise grows
   position std only to ~0.1 mm at 80 ms — the prediction is *very* good
   for short horizons; the ellipse mainly reflects GNSS fusion quality.
3. The velocity std grows from 0.037 → 0.147 m/s over the horizon (the
   acceleration uncertainty accumulates) — the dominant covariance
   growth channel.

## Links to user's domain

- The uncertainty ellipse is exactly what an AR-HUD needs to fade/clamp
  markers when the display-time pose confidence drops (e.g. GNSS
  multipath in urban canyons, turn events).
- The model-error term is tunable per use-case: conservative (0.5 m/s²)
  vs aggressive (0.2 m/s²) — a natural knob for the demo/pilot phase.
- The 9×9 block extraction (`eskf.state.P[:9, :9]`) makes the ESKF and
  the predictor a closed loop: fused covariance in, display-time
  confidence out.
