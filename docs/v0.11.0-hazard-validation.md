# v0.11.0 — Hazard Assessment & Warning Arbitration

**Module:** `sensor_sim/hazard.py` · **Demo:** `examples/hazard_demo.py`
**Tests:** `tests/test_hazard.py` (+11, total 100 passing)

## Problem (the decision layer v0.9/v0.10 left open)

v0.9.0 `MarkerProjector` / `AdasPipeline` put ADAS markers (ACC lead box, FCW/AEB
hazard, lane lines) on the HUD at the right place and time. v0.10.0
`MarkerVisibilityPolicy` decided whether a marker should be drawn FULL / FADED /
CULLED from FOV, near-field angular oversize and bodywork occlusion. Neither
decides **what to tell the driver**: how urgent is each hazard, and if several
are present, which one claims the single headline warning slot that a real HUD
only has one of.

## Design

| Piece | Role |
|---|---|
| `TTCModel` | Time-to-collision from display-time range + closing rate. Constant-relative-speed (the ISO 15623 FCW conservative floor) and constant-relative-deceleration (the AEB-style richer model). Range +∞-safe; gates threats out beyond `gating_range_m`. |
| `HazardAssessment` | Normalizes one marker + kinematics into a threat score in [0,1]. Blends (a) TTC vs a model horizon, (b) marker-kind urgency weight (hazard > lead_vehicle > lane), (c) cross-track/bearing alignment so off-axis markers decay toward advisory before they can arbitrate. |
| `WarningArbitrator` | Across all markers, selects the single most threatening as the headline warning, mapped to a discrete `WarningLevel` (OFF / CAUTION / WARN / CRITICAL / EMERGENCY). CULLED / fully-faded markers (v0.10) cannot claim the slot. |
| `ThreatPipeline` | End-to-end: reuses the marker-render path and computes the *warning level* under both the naive (latency-lagged) and display-time-compensated poses — answering whether uncompensated latency flips the *safety decision*, not just pixel error. |

## Validation

- **TTC correctness:** 50 m gap closing at 20 m/s → TTC 2.5 s. Not-closing,
  out-of-range, and behind cases return `None`. Constant-deceleration TTC is
  strictly more urgent than the constant-speed floor when a sustained
  deceleration applies.
- **Latency flips the decision:** ego at 25 m/s, 0.2 s camera/pipeline delay,
  a slow-closing (2 m/s) hazard 12 m ahead (7 m true gap at display time):
  - **naive** fused pose (5 m behind truth) reads the hazard at 12 m → **CRITICAL**
  - **compensated** pose (≈truth) reads it at 7 m → **EMERGENCY**
  - `level_changed=True`, `level_delta=+1`. A real system without the v0.5/v0.9
    display-time compensation would *under-warn* the driver in exactly the
    situation AEB exists for.
- **Arbitration:** a close fast hazard wins over a distant ACC lead and the
  lane edges; a CULLED/off-axis marker cannot claim the headline slot (its
  threat is gated to 0 / level OFF).
- **Warning ladder:** OFF→CAUTION→WARN→CRITICAL→EMERGENCY verified via
  `level_from_score` thresholds.

## Production lessons (for AR-HUD)

1. **Compute TTC on display-time (compensated) range, never the raw fused range**
   — uncompensated latency reports the ego behind truth, so the hazard looks
   farther than it is and the HUD **under-claims** urgency (a would-be
   EMERGENCY is read as CRITICAL).
2. **Gate warnings on the v0.10 visibility decision** — a CULLED/fully-faded
   marker (behind, too close, out of FOV, or bodywork-occluded) must not claim
   the headline slot.
3. **Cross-track / bearing alignment matters** — an FCW wedge a few degrees
   off-axis in an adjacent lane is an advisory, not an alert. Decay the threat
   by azimuth before it can arbitrate.
4. **Choose the constant-deceleration TTC for AEB decisions** but only for
   genuinely fast closures; the constant-speed model is the honest FCW floor
   for slow-closing lead traffic (a panicking −9 m/s² relative decel on a
   2 m/s closure over-claims).
5. **Arbitration is single-slot** — less-urgent markers stay drawn but quiet;
   a distant lane-edge advisory must not override a real collision threat.
